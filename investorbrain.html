The Premise

Capital is not the constraint. Judgment is.

A billion-dollar allocator and a broke beginner face the exact same decision every day: where does the next unit of scarce resource go, and why? For the allocator that unit is a dollar. For you, right now, it's an hour of attention. The operating system is identical — the allocator just runs it with more zeros.

MIDAS is that operating system, made explicit and interactive. Four pillars, a set of faculties that feed them, and tools you can actually run against a real decision.

At these numbers, a rational allocator would take this bet repeatedly — the expected value is strongly positive and the upside is many times the downside.

  • Expected value = p × upside − (1−p) × downside. Positive means the bet pays over many repetitions.
  • Payoff ratio = upside ÷ downside. Allocators want this large so they can be wrong often and still win.
  • Kelly size = the fraction of capital that maximizes long-run growth. Most pros bet a fraction of it (see below).

Educational model, not investment advice. Real bets have fat tails, correlation, and unknown probabilities — which is exactly why the next two pillars exist.

Advanced Faculties

The deeper machinery

The four pillars are the spine. These are the advanced organs an allocator runs on top of them — Bayesian belief-updating, survival simulation, time-arbitrage, inversion red-teaming, and a bias firewall. This is where amateurs and allocators visibly diverge.

Advanced 01 · feeds Pillar 02

Bayesian Base-Rate Updater

The single most common amateur error is base-rate neglect — falling in love with a story and ignoring how often bets like this actually work. Start from the reference class, then let your edge move the number a disciplined amount. Push the result straight into the engine.

If your signal is no better at spotting winners than losers (TPR ≈ FPR), it carries zero information — the posterior collapses back to the base rate. That gap is your edge.

The Reference Library

Operating principles the machine runs on

The mental models the greatest allocators return to again and again — the source code behind the four pillars.

Margin of Safety

Buy a dollar for fifty cents

Only act when the gap between price and value is so wide that even a wrong estimate still leaves you whole. The margin is the risk management.

Inversion

Solve it backwards

Don't ask how to win — ask what guarantees you lose, then relentlessly avoid that. "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there."

Second-Level Thinking

Not "good company" — "better than priced"

First-level thinking sees a great business. Second-level asks what everyone else already believes and whether the price reflects it. Edge lives in the gap.

Convexity / Barbell

Bounded loss, unbounded gain

Structure exposure so the worst case is capped and the best case is open-ended. Take many small convex bets; let optionality do the heavy lifting.

Contrarian Truth

What important truth do few agree with?

The best asymmetric bets look wrong to the consensus right up until they're obviously right. Being early and alone is the price of the outsized payoff.

Reflexivity

Perception bends the fundamentals

Prices don't just reflect reality — they change it. Watch the feedback loop between belief and fact; the trend and the turn both live there.

Circle of Competence

Know the edge of what you know

The size of the circle matters far less than knowing exactly where its boundary is. Outside it, you don't have a bet — you have a guess.

Radical Open-Mindedness

Stress-test to find truth

Hunt for the smartest people who disagree and try to see what they see. Believability-weight the input. Ego is a tax on returns.

Time Arbitrage

Patience is the last free edge

As information and speed get arbitraged away, willingness to wait longer than everyone else is the one advantage that keeps compounding.

$0 · 72 Hours

The build protocol

You can't manufacture a track record in three days. You can wire the cognitive machine so that when opportunity and capital arrive, you already think like the allocator who deserves them.

Hour
0–8

Define the circle & the reference class

Write down the 2–3 domains you can genuinely judge better than average. For each, collect the base rates — how these bets usually pay off. No edge without a reference class.

Hour
8–24

Build the asymmetry filter

Codify the single rule: capped downside, open-ended upside, and a payoff ratio you'd take repeatedly. Run ten live situations through the Asymmetry Engine above. Reject nine.

Hour
24–48

Install the sizing & survival math

Adopt fractional-Kelly sizing as a permanent rule. Set a hard "never risk more than X% on one thesis" line so no single wrong call can end the game. Survival first, growth second.

Hour
48–66

Engineer patience

Open the conviction journal. Write your first real thesis with its invalidation trigger. Set the default to "do nothing" and pre-commit the exits. Automate the discipline you can't be trusted to feel.

Hour
66–72

Run the pre-mortem & ship

Assume every bet failed — write why. Fix what you can, accept what you can't, and start compounding decisions. The track record is just this loop, run honestly, for years.
